Game World: Westeros (Realm of Thrones)
The known world is vast. To the north lies the frozen land beyond the Wall, in the south — the deserts of Dorne, in the west — the Westerlands and coastline, and to the east — the Dothraki Sea and the Free Cities of Essos. Magic exists in this world in rare and ancient forms — dragons, shadowbinders, and the power of the Lord of Light. The population consists entirely of humans, though legends speak of the Children of the Forest and the White Walkers beyond the Wall.

There are several great kingdoms and noble houses in Westeros, all nominally united under the Iron Throne in King's Landing:
– House Baratheon of King's Landing: Rules the Seven Kingdoms from the Iron Throne. Their words are "Ours is the Fury."
– House Lannister of Casterly Rock: The wealthiest house in Westeros, rulers of the Westerlands. Their words are "Hear Me Roar," though their unofficial motto is "A Lannister always pays his debts."
– House Stark of Winterfell: The ancient rulers of the North. Their words are "Winter is Coming."
– House Tully of Riverrun: Rulers of the Riverlands. Their words are "Family, Duty, Honor."
– House Arryn of the Eyrie: Rulers of the Vale. Their words are "As High as Honor."
– House Tyrell of Highgarden: Rulers of the Reach, the most fertile lands in Westeros. Their words are "Growing Strong."
– House Martell of Sunspear: Rulers of Dorne, the southernmost kingdom. Their words are "Unbowed, Unbent, Unbroken."
– House Greyjoy of Pyke: Rulers of the Iron Islands. Their words are "We Do Not Sow."
– House Targaryen: The former dragonlord dynasty, once rulers of all Westeros. Now in exile or hiding.
– House Velaryon of Driftmark: An ancient house with strong ties to the sea and to House Targaryen.
– House Dragonstone: Holds the ancestral seat of House Targaryen.
– The Night's Watch: An ancient order that guards the Wall against threats from the north. They take no part in the politics of the realm.

The world is governed by feudalism: great lords rule over their lands and bannermen. Society is largely agrarian; most smallfolk live and work on farms. The continent is dotted with castles and fortresses that serve as centers of power and defense.

The economy of Westeros is based on trade and agriculture. The primary currency is gold dragons, with silver stags and copper pennies also in use. The Lannisters control the gold mines of Casterly Rock, making them the wealthiest house.

Essos is the vast continent to the east, across the Narrow Sea. It includes the Free Cities — Braavos, Pentos, Myr, Tyrosh, Lys, Volantis, Qohor, Lorath, and Norvos — as well as the Dothraki Sea, Slaver's Bay, and the ancient ruins of Valyria. Merchants, sellswords, scholars, and exiles travel between Westeros and Essos regularly. The Free Cities are wealthy and powerful in their own right, and many Westerosi nobles have connections, debts, or enemies across the Narrow Sea.

Lords and ladies do not simply join strangers on adventures. The world is dangerous: bandits, outlaws, and hostile armies roam the countryside and threaten travelers.

Additional Information About Westeros:
– The Wall is a massive structure of ice in the far north, built thousands of years ago to keep out the White Walkers and the wildlings (Free Folk) who live beyond it.
– The Iron Throne, forged from the swords of Aegon the Conqueror's enemies, is said to cut those who are unworthy of sitting upon it.
– Ravens are used throughout Westeros to carry messages between castles and keeps. A maester at every castle tends to the ravens and serves as advisor to the lord.
– The Faith of the Seven is the dominant religion in most of Westeros. The North still follows the Old Gods of the Forest. The Iron Islands worship the Drowned God.
– Tournaments are held across the realm — jousting, melee combat, and archery contests draw knights and spectators from across the land.
– Valyrian steel is an extremely rare and precious metal, forged in the fires of old Valyria using dragonfire and sorcery. Only a handful of Valyrian steel blades exist in Westeros.

Known Scripted Events in the Realm:
– The Siege of Harrenhal: Harrenhal is one of the largest and most cursed castles in Westeros. It was destroyed by Aegon the Conqueror's dragon Balerion during the War of Conquest. Military conflict around Harrenhal is of great historical and strategic significance to the Riverlands.
– The Battle of the Bastards: A legendary and brutal battle. A great clash between rival claimants, fought with tremendous losses on both sides. NPCs speak of it with awe, dread, or celebration depending on their allegiances.
– The Night King's Duel: The threat from beyond the Wall. The Night King leads the White Walkers and their army of the dead. His confrontation with the living is spoken of in hushed, fearful tones across the realm.
– Storyline Wars: The great political and military conflicts between the noble houses shape the fate of the realm. These wars shift alliances, change rulers, and redraw the borders of power.

Daily Life in Westeros and Essos:
The medieval era was marked by feudalism, with lords ruling over land and bannermen. Most smallfolk lived in small villages and worked as laborers, farmers, or craftsmen. Urban life in cities like King's Landing or the Free Cities of Essos focused on trade and commerce.
Life centered on survival and sustenance; food was the main concern for most. Clothing was often handmade and simple, while the wealthy wore rich fabrics and fine armor. Education was limited to the nobility and the maesters of the Citadel.
Entertainment included feasts, tournaments, jousting, and melee combat. Medicine was primitive — maesters provided what healing they could. Travel was difficult and dangerous. Knights, sellswords, and men-at-arms served lords in exchange for pay. In Essos, trade galleys crossed the Narrow Sea regularly, and sellsword companies were available for hire in most Free Cities.
