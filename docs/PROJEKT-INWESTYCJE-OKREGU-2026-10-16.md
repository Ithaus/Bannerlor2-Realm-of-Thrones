## chosen

MERGED DESIGN "District works" (Polish name in logs: Prace okregu, PO). The core is Design 2, with parts taken from Designs 1 and 3. Part A (side-village crafts, district sum, icon) is shared by all three designs and is kept with two fixes.

How I judged the three:
- Design 1, a stake in the craft.
  - Good: closed money, simple pricing.
  - Fails the brief: it does NOT raise production, and Jeff said in so many words "zwieksza to produkcje".
  - Its income comes out of the village purse, which is the peasants' food money. Its 1/12 lord fee has no source in the research.
  - Rejected as the core. Kept from it: loss per emptied settlement (no cliff at 50%), auto-rebuild that keeps a wage reserve, the option in both menus, the save and log layout.
- Design 2, works. It fits Jeff's words best:
  - the coin goes to people (wages to the village purse, real goods bought at the town market);
  - one village really makes more of its own good, and half of what the extra sells for is the investor's;
  - a raid destroys exactly that capital, depending on its kind; herds are driven off to the raider as goods;
  - the asset is lasting, with diminishing returns.
  Fixed in this merge:
  - first-coin return in a side village lowered from 32% to 26% (bonus 0.30 instead of 0.40), so typical returns of 19-24% sit between folk loans (12%) and the Iron Bank (20-45%);
  - loss is proportional to settlements emptied, instead of everything lost at the 50% "burned" line;
  - the kit price follows today's market instead of being frozen at the first buyer's price;
  - AI investing is not on by default: it is stage 4 and the test decides;
  - the lord's takeover of unpaid rebuilds is dropped.
- Design 3, a contract.
  - Good: the most exact bookkeeping.
  - Too complex for the player: terms, renewals, a risk rate with 6 parts.
  - Too complex for the code: dues per good, earmarks inside the village tax code. It also has a blind spot for raids from the sea.
  - It expires, so it is not "an investment in the area".
  - Kept from it: a separate baked crafts file (the layout file and its CRC stay untouched, so no save remap), the start-type order with fallbacks per zone, +1 relation with notables, and the payout taken from the lord's counter and the village purse in proportion, so nobody pays out of the old harvest.
- Part A fixes:
  1. The icon is the game's own primary good (VillageType.PrimaryProduction) of the craft that earns the district the most money. That is always one of the 23 native sprites, and cattle districts do not flip from cow to butter.
  2. A village that holds works keeps its craft.

Correction to Designs 1 and 2: they said the whole district loses yield while refugees are away. That is wrong. Output is u_v x Y = home/73,020, so each village's output follows its own people at home. Only the emptied villages lose output.

## rules

A. EACH SIDE VILLAGE HAS ITS OWN CRAFT; THE DISTRICT IS THE SUM (Jeff 16.10, binding)
A0. Prerequisite: fix HamletKinds W2 first.
- The line "Wioski wedlug rodzaju (210)" showed "okregow z rybakami 0, ryb +0.0" in all 125 daily lines of 15-16.10.
- Daily() reads records stamped yesterday (day = today - 1). If the BK production tick runs inside the same daily tick before ArmouryBehavior.OnDailyTick, every record carries today's stamp, and the line counts 0.
- First check with a one-day probe whether goods really moved (fish in the stores of districts with fishing hamlets) or only the log misses them. Then fix.
- The craft step reuses W2's list step, its amount step and its control (goods removed = goods added).
A1. Baked file ModuleData/arm_village_crafts.tsv, one row per side-village uid (1957 rows, layout v5):
- zone, ground (snow / sand / steppe / forest / mountain / swamp / grass), river / bridge / ford / coast / lake, d_sea, d_river, d_mount;
- candidate crafts as "type:w" with w >= 0.5;
- the start craft.
It is generated from a-teren/teren.npz plus arm_map_villages.tsv by a deterministic script in the repo's tools/. arm_map_villages.tsv and its crc do not change. No file means no crafts, which is today's behaviour.
A2. Suitability w = zone weight x ground factor x water factor. The tables are the ZW / TW / water_mult tables of symulacja_upraw.py.
- Snow sets every crop to 0. So: no olives, vines, wheat, flax, cotton or dates in the snow.
- Sand: no lumber, flax or swine.
- Mines only next to mountains (ground mountain or d_mount <= 2) in mining districts.
- Horse ranching only in ranch districts, on grass or steppe.
A3. Start craft, the first rule that applies:
1. picture "fishing": fisherman (whaler in whaler districts);
2. mill, windmill or granary: wheat, if it is a candidate;
3. the district type, if it is a candidate;
4. the best candidate;
5. a fallback for the zone: snow = trapper, else sheep; sand without a river = date, then olive, then fisherman on the coast; tropics = cotton, then fisherman.
A crop never starts on snow.
A4. The main (game) village always keeps the district type, so the BK class, mines, warehouse and workshops stay tied to it.
A5. Output of each part:
- q_w(g) = standing-settlement share x rate(craft, g) x u_v x g'(craft) x winter x buildings x regulator x Y x L, plus the works bonus (B).
- Mechanism: a generalised W2 step in MaterialLaw.ProdPostfix (last step).
  - The district-type goods get a factor (1 - share of the side villages that have other crafts).
  - The goods of the other crafts are added as base amounts (W2's fish pattern), and are put on the BK list with weight 0 (MineralOnce list step).
  - The warehouse cap grows the way VillageFruit.CapPostfix does it.
- A side village fishes or farms with all its hands. The kitchen grain 3 of its own type stays.
A6. Neutral switch-on. On the first day the key is on, g'(T) = g(T) x world labour of T before / after. It is saved (arm_crafts).
- Every type's world total on day one equals the day before. After that, only profit moves it.
- Expected corrections: wheat x0.76, fisherman x0.78, olive x0.84, cattle x1.21, clay x1.44, swine x1.61, silk x1.70.
A7. Switching on profit, every 28 days (the HarvestValve cycle):
- score = w x sqrt(price index of the craft's goods at the bound town, weighted by base value), the E3-D1 rule;
- a village switches when the best score >= 1.25 x the score of its current craft;
- at most 5% of the world's labour of a type moves per cycle, largest gains first;
- vines, olives and dates ramp up to full output over 364 days;
- a village where anyone holds works never switches.
A8. District value V_d = the sum over all parts at 28-day cart prices (fallback: Value x the town's index).
A9. Icon. The craft with the largest summed value in the district (main village plus all side villages) gives the nameplate its game primary good (VillageType.PrimaryProduction of that type). It changes only when the new craft beats the shown one by 10%. It is recomputed at session start and every 28-day cycle.
- Expected: the icon changes in 81 of 558 districts. Most common: grape to grain 18, clay to grain 12, hardwood to grain 6.
- The hover "Primary production" line and the town's production list show the same good.

B. DISTRICT WORKS (the investment)
B1. From the main village's menu, the player picks ONE part of the district (the main village or one side village) and pays for works for that part's craft. Kinds:
- BOATS (fisherman, whaler, walrus): boats, nets, drying racks.
- FIELDS (wheat, flax, silk/cotton): plough teams, mill, barns.
- HERD (cattle, sheep, swine): the animals and pens.
- TREES (vineyard, olive, date): new rows, terraces, a press.
- PITS (iron, silver, salt, clay): shafts, timbering, pans, kilns.
- WOODS (lumberjack, trapper): saws, sledges, traps.
- Not in v1:
  - horse ranching: village carts show no horse sales (check first);
  - the 11 Night's Watch gift villages: their output is flat.
B2. Price.
- A full kit for one homestead (settlement) is one year of what that homestead makes of its craft, at today's 28-day cart prices, normal season, regulator 1, full yield.
- Buying K gold gives K / kit-price "kit units". One unit = one homestead fully equipped.
B3. Bonus b = B x (2x - x^2), where x = working units / standing homesteads of the part (capped at 1).
- B = 0.30 in a side village, 0.20 in the main village (the lord's mill and demesne ploughs are already there).
- Units count as they are built: linearly over the kind's ready time (herd 7 days, woods 14, boats / fields / pits 28, trees 182).
B4. The extra output is b x the part's craft output. It passes through winter, the regulator, people at home and the hands factor, like everything else.
B5. Income.
- At every return of the district's cart home, half of the share of the takings that the extra output makes up is the investors'.
- It is taken from the lord's counter part and the village purse part of that return, in proportion. BK estates keep their cut.
- It is paid to the holder clan's leader.
- No carts on raided or plundered days means no pay. Food bought straight inside the village pays the investor nothing.
B6. Limits:
- a part can be equipped at most to a full kit (all holders together);
- minimum purchase 200;
- the room offered = (homesteads - all units held) x the kit price.
B7. When you can buy:
- the village is not at war with your faction, and not BeingRaided or Looted;
- the part is standing or damaged (not burning, burned or abandoned);
- the callback re-checks your gold before paying.
B8. Loss in raids, rebuilding and abandoned homesteads: see the burning section.
B9. War: while the holder's faction is at war with the district's faction, nothing is paid (that coin stays in the normal split). The works stay and keep producing.
B10. The holder is the clan; an heir inherits. If the clan dies out, its units become the village's: they keep working, and their extra goes to the normal split.
- Change of owner or conquest: nothing changes.
- If you own the district, you also get the lord's tax as usual.
B11. No selling of works in v1. The works are sunk into the village, like a real improvement.
B12. Relation: +1 with the district's notables when your works there pass 1% of the district's yearly production, at most once per 28 days. No coin moves.
B13. AI lords (stage 4, key DistrictWorksAi, OFF until its test passes). The rule is written for any clan; their behaviour comes from current profit:
- every 28 days, each landed clan leader looks at his own districts;
- purse at least 60 days of his party wages + 20,000;
- the target part is standing, with no raid in the region for 28 days;
- the next coin must earn >= 20% a year at today's season and regulator, which in practice means only goods the world lacks and only outside winter;
- amount = min(5% of the gold above the reserve, the room up to where that return falls to 20%);
- world pace <= 0.5% of the world's yearly village production per 28 days.

C. BEE
- With DistrictWorks on, BeeSeal hides bee_village_invest_5k/15k/30k (MenuGate: visible false). It adds "Invest in the district's works" to bee_village_menu before bee_village_back (BeeGifts.AddGiftOption pattern). The same option is in the vanilla village menu, so it works without BEE.
- The CanPlayerInvest postfix stays, so the BK Demesne screen and the BK console stay refused, with the reworded text.
- LordInvestmentReserveFlat = 1e9 stays. BEE state is never written.
- Key off: exactly today's game. Works stay in the save, frozen: no bonus, no pay.

## formulas

Notation: w = a part of district d (the main village or one side village). N_w = homesteads (250 people each). E_w = empty homesteads (raid Br + famine Bf). S_w = N_w - E_w = standing homesteads.

F1 Output of good g in part w:
q_w(g) = (S_w / S_d) x rate(T_w, g) x u_d x g'(T_w) x winter_g x reg_g x Y_d x L_d x bld_d x (1 + b_w if g is one of the craft's goods)
- u_d x Y_d = home / 73,020, so each part's output follows its own people at home.
F2 District production value: V_d = sum over w and g of q_w(g) x p_g (+ village woodlot). Per year: x 364.
- p_g = 28-day mean cart price (GoodsLedger). Fallback: item Value x the town's index.
F3 Craft value of a part, without the bonus: v_w = sum over the craft goods G(T_w) of q_w(g) / (1 + b_w) x p_g.
- G(T) = the type's production list (game + NavalDLC + BK) minus the kitchen grain of non-wheat types.
F4 Kit price per homestead: c_w = 364 x sum over G(T_w) of rate(T_w, g) x (250 / 73,020) x g'(T_w) x bld_d x p_g.
- Normal season, regulator 1, full yield, hands 1. Full kit of a part = N_w x c_w.
F5 Purchase: units = K / c_w, ready linearly over the kind's ready days.
F6 Fill: x_w = min(1, sum of working units U_w / S_w).
F7 Bonus: b_w = B_w x (2x - x^2). B = 0.30 side village, 0.20 main village.
F8 Extra value per day: E_w = b_w x v_w. Holder h: E_hw = E_w x U_hw / U_w.
F9 Pool at each cart return home with handed gold T: P = s x (sum of E_w / V_d) x T, with s = 0.5.
- Holder h gets P x E_h / sum of E.
- Taken from the lord's counter: P x tax / (tax + village); from the village purse: P x village / (tax + village). That is 40.9% / 59.1% with the 112 split of 38.2 / 55.2 / 6.7.
- P <= 0.5 x T always. The lord and the village each keep at least what they would get without works.
F10 Expected income shown: I_h = 364 x s x H_d x E_h, where H_d = the district's 28-day handed takings / V_d. World mean for non-horse districts: 271,263 / (334,330 - 21,966) = 0.868.
F11 Returns at purchase (normal season, regulator 1):
- average yield = s x H x B x (2 - x);
- marginal yield (next coin) = s x H x B x 2 x (1 - x).
- Side village, x = 0 / 0.25 / 0.5 / 0.75 / 1:
  - average 26.1 / 22.8 / 19.5 / 16.3 / 13.0%;
  - marginal 26.1 / 19.5 / 13.0 / 6.5 / 0%.
- Main village, average 17.4 / 15.2 / 13.0 / 10.9 / 8.7%.
- Today's regulator multiplies these: goods at x1.30 earn more (cattle 27.7% because cows are x1.30). Winter cuts them (about -40%).
F12 Shares shown:
- of the village's craft output: s x b / (1 + b) x U_h / U_w;
- of the district's production: s x E_h / V_d.
F13 Room: (N_w - sum of (U + idle + rebuildable + ramping)) x c_w.
- "Sensible" limit = the fill where the marginal yield falls to 12% (the folk-loan rate): x = 0.54 to 0.57 in a side village, 0.31 to 0.35 in the main village.
F14 Burning:
- When raid-empty homesteads rise by dE (with S_before standing): moved = U x dE / S_before.
  - lost Z += L_kind x moved;
  - idle I += (1 - L_kind) x moved.
- Famine flight: I += moved.
- When dE' homesteads return (E_before empty): back to work = I x dE' / E_before; rebuildable now = Z x dE' / E_before.
- Rebuild cost = R_kind x c_w x units. The deadline is 91 days after the return.
F15 Neutral switch-on: g'(T) = g(T) x Lab_T(all side villages = district type) / Lab_T(start crafts), where Lab = the sum of u x labour share.
F16 Craft score: w_T x sqrt(I_T), with I_T = sum(rate x Value x index) / sum(rate x Value) at the bound town. Switch when best >= 1.25 x current.
F17 Icon: craft* = argmax over T of the sum over parts with T_w = T of the sum over G(T) of q_w(g) x p_g. Icon = PrimaryProduction(craft*). It changes when >= 1.10 x the shown craft.

## burning

PRINCIPLE: works are spread evenly over a part's standing homesteads. Every homestead emptied by raiders takes its share of the works with it. No coin is destroyed: tools, boats and buildings were real goods bought at the market and are now ashes, and the animals go to the raider as goods.

KIND TABLE (constants in code, written into the session-start line)
Columns: materials share m (the rest is wages) / ready days / lost per emptied homestead L / rebuild price R as a share of today's kit price.
- HERD (cattle, sheep, swine): m 0.60 (the type's own animal items) / 7 / L 1.00 / R 1.00. The animals are driven off into the raider party's roster, as many as were bought for those homesteads; with no raider party, into the village store. You buy them again at full price.
- BOATS (fisherman, whaler, walrus): m 0.35 (hardwood, flax, tools) / 28 / L 1.00 / R 0.50. Hulls and nets burn; anchors and iron are saved.
- FIELDS (wheat, flax, silk/cotton): m 0.30 (tools, hardwood, limestone for millstones) / 28 / L 0.50 / R 0.50. The mill and barns burn; the fields and ditches stay. The other half sits idle until people return.
- TREES (vineyard, olive, date): m 0.15 / 182 / L 0.25 / R 0.50. Only the press and some rows are lost; the trees survive.
- PITS (iron, silver, salt, clay): m 0.35 / 28 / L 0.50 / R 0.50. Timbering and winches burn; the shafts stay.
- WOODS (lumberjack, trapper): m 0.25 / 14 / L 1.00 / R 0.50.

RULES
1. When: at each raid strike (DistrictRaid E7/R1, settlement by settlement) and in the daily pass. Count only raid-empty homesteads (Br; army foraging, Devastation.Strike, also counts). Apply moved = U x dE / S_before: L x moved is lost (it can be rebuilt), the rest sits idle.
2. Famine flight (Bf) destroys nothing. The works in those homesteads sit idle and start working again when people return.
3. Fill per standing homestead does not change in a raid. Income from the burned part falls by the share of homesteads emptied, because output follows the people at home. Other parts are untouched.
4. Burn order (unchanged): the side village facing the enemy first, the nearest next, the main village last. That is why the main village pays less.
5. While a part is burning or burned (50% or more empty, held down to 47%), nobody can buy new works there. Rebuilding is allowed as homesteads return.
6. Return: refugees start back after 30 quiet days and are all home within 364 days. As homesteads return:
   - idle works start working again on their own;
   - lost works can be rebuilt at R x today's kit price, within 91 days of that homestead's return.
7. Auto-rebuild (default ON, a switch in the window) pays at once, as long as 30 days of party wages stay in the purse. Otherwise the cost waits in the window ("Rebuild 3 homesteads: 1,500"), with a reminder 14 days before the deadline.
8. When the deadline passes, the right is dropped. Those homesteads have no works, the fill falls, and so does the income. New works cost the full price.
9. Raiding a village where you hold works burns your own works. With herds you get your own animals back; the wages are lost.
10. A burned part keeps its craft (it is frozen while it holds works).
11. The main village going Looted is not a special case: its homesteads are emptied last, through the same per-homestead rule.
12. Loot of the purse and the store, refugees, deaths and captives are exactly as today (DistrictRaid / Devastation).

## money_routes

Every coin has a payer and a receiver. The BEE sink (GiveGoldAction to null) stays closed.

1. BUYING (player, or an AI clan leader in stage 4). Two parts:
   a. Wages, (1 - m) x K: the buyer pays the district's village purse (GiveGoldAction.ApplyForCharacterToSettlement to the game village). Ledger: "prace okregu: place do kiesy wsi".
   b. Materials, m x K: the buyer pays the market purse of the bound town (kasa targu), for real pieces taken off its shelf through ShelfBuy.Take at no more than BuildMaterialPriceCap (2.0) x value. The pieces leave the world and become the works. Ledger: "prace okregu: material do kasy targu".
      - A castle-bound district buys at the nearest town, as BuildFunding does.
      - Short shelf: next from the village's own store (pieces), paid to the village purse.
      - Anything still short is paid as wages to the village purse: the villagers make it themselves.
2. INCOME at each return of the district's cart home (VillageTakings.EnterPostfix, after Whole112, CoalFirst and TaxCounterGuard):
   - the pool P is taken from the lord's tax counter (Village.TradeTaxAccumulated), about 41%, and from the village purse, about 59%, and goes to the holder clan's leader;
   - the coin came from the town purse that bought the goods (the existing 112 chain);
   - BK estates keep their cut; P is never more than half of the handed gold;
   - ledger: "prace okregu: wyplata z licznika pana" and "wyplata z kiesy wsi".
3. WAR between the holder and the district: no flow. The coin stays in the normal split.
4. BURN: no coin moves. Tools and boats are destroyed (the goods already left the world at purchase). Animals move into the raider party's roster as goods. Purse loot follows the existing rule.
5. REBUILD: the same routes as buying, at R x price.
6. Expired rebuild right, or holder clan extinct: no coin moves. The works pass to the village.
7. Who gains from the extra output E, per unit of its sale value after the toll:
   - investor 0.500;
   - lord 0.382 - 0.205 = 0.177;
   - village purse 0.552 - 0.295 = 0.257;
   - BK estates 0.067.
   Plus the 4% toll on the cart (half to the market town's lord, half to the crown). Nobody gets less than without works.
8. After that the coin follows existing paths. Wages in the village purse are spent on purchases, and the part above the normal level is hit by the existing surplus tax (0.67 to the lord, the rest to crown, castle or town). If the player owns the district, he gets part of his own wages back through that tax; this is the lord's existing right, and it is logged.
9. Control in the daily line:
   - paid in = wages + materials (difference 0);
   - paid out = from counters + from purses (difference 0);
   - payout <= pool <= 0.5 x handed in every window (0 violations);
   - MoneyLedger "bez odbiorcy" (no receiver) stays 0.
